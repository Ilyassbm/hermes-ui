# 90-second demo script

Record at 1440×900, dark theme, one bot connected with a few older conversations. Keep the cursor moves calm; the
point is that the page never stumbles.

| Time | On screen | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:08 | The page opens on the bots rail. Pinned bot on top, team below. | "This is Hermes UI: a workspace for Hermes that replaces the native home. One rail for the team, one thread, one panel for what the conversation produced." |
| 0:08–0:18 | Hover two bot rows: each shows its last line and how long ago. | "Every bot keeps its own model, its own effort, and its last output — so you know who to ask before you ask." |
| 0:18–0:30 | Click a bot, then type a prompt with an image attached. Send. | "Attach an image and it goes with the prompt: a thumbnail in your own message, and the model actually sees it." |
| 0:30–0:45 | The live line appears: "thinking", then "runs a command · 3s". Stop appears; let it finish, the line folds to "Worked for 21s". | "While it works, you see what it is doing and for how long — not a spinner. Stop ends the turn and the line folds instead of disappearing." |
| 0:45–0:58 | Open the reasoning fold, then the right panel showing the files the conversation touched. Click one: preview. | "The reasoning stays folded until you want it. And everything the turn produced is in the right panel, previewable." |
| 0:58–1:10 | Type `/` in the composer: the palette filters as you type. Pick `/help`, Enter: the answer lands in the thread. | "Slash commands are real commands, not text: they run and their answer lands in the thread." |
| 1:10–1:22 | ⌥2 to jump to another bot, ⌘⇧O for a new chat, ⌘⌥B to fold the panel. Usage meters at the foot. | "Keyboard first: a digit jumps to a bot, ⌘⇧O starts a fresh conversation, and your subscriptions' limits sit at the foot of the rail." |
| 1:22–1:30 | Settings ▸ Plugins ▸ Hermes UI: theme, density, width. Switch light, back to dark. | "It is a plugin, not a skin: MIT, installable from the catalogue, with its own settings page — light, dark, density, reading width." |

Tag `@NousResearch` in the post. Keep the video under 90 seconds — the judges score on usefulness, reliability,
polish and creativity, and they favour plugins that do more than a prompt or a skill could.
